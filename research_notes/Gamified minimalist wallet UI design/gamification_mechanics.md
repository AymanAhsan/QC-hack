# Gamification Mechanics in Money Apps: What Helps, What Hurts, and What Fits a Card-Rewards Optimizer

Scope note: the research covers consumer mobile apps, with US/UK regulation, mostly from 2018–2026. Older evidence is flagged. Several product pages and press sites returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool (Bayes Business School, CNBC, Finextra, Washington Times/AP). For those, findings come from search-result summaries of the page and are marked "(snippet)". I read the FCA 2024 research note in full from its PDF, along with the Duolingo blog posts and the PLOS ONE leaderboard paper.

## 1. How real products implement gamification (mechanic + presentation)

### Takeaway
Two groups emerge. The durable, well-regarded mechanics reward a behavior the user already wants and forgive lapses: Duolingo's streak with freezes, Apple's pausable rings, Qapital's rules, Acorns' round-ups. The mechanics that drew regulatory action or public backlash either tie celebrations or chance-based prizes to transaction frequency (Robinhood confetti and scratch tickets, Revolut prize draws, Fetch's spin wheel) or turn users' own spending data into mockery (Monzo's "savage" Year in Monzo).

### Cited Findings

**Duolingo (streaks, freezes, XP/leagues, hearts to energy)**
- In 2020 Duolingo separated the streak from the daily XP goal. Since then, a single lesson extends the streak and goal progress is shown separately. Results (relative): +3.3% Day-14 retention, +1% daily active learners, +10.5% daily learners on a streak after 20 days, +19% for new learners, and "over 40%" more learners on a 7+ day streak. Duolingo named the trade-off: "fewer learners were actually reaching their daily goals without the motivation of the streak." (Post dated Nov 19, 2020, by Anton Yu.) — [Duolingo Blog: Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- Raising the number of Streak Freezes a learner can equip at once to two "actually increased the relative number of active learners on Duolingo every day by +0.38%," instead of encouraging more days off. A new streak-milestone animation raised Day-7 retention for new learners by +1.7%. — [Duolingo Blog: streak research (2024)](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/)
- The same post names loss aversion as the mechanism: not wanting to lose a long streak keeps people practicing on days they would skip. It also concedes that losing a streak can demotivate, and that people may avoid starting a challenge at all to escape the disappointment of breaking it. It cites research that a little slack motivates better than rigid rules, and claims learners with a 7-day streak are 3.6x more likely to finish a course (correlational; no baseline given). — [Duolingo Blog: streak research](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/)
- Duolingo reportedly ran 600+ experiments on the streak over four years (secondary summary of a Lenny's Podcast interview with Jackson Shuttleworth, Group PM, Retention). — [recall.it summary of Lenny's Podcast](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)
- In 2025 Duolingo began replacing "hearts" (a penalty for mistakes) with "energy." Every exercise uses energy, and runs of correct answers refill it. Duolingo framed this as rewarding correct answers rather than punishing mistakes. Some users strongly objected: one writer quit after a 700+ day streak, and a petition claims climbing leagues became nearly impossible without a paid subscription (petition claim, not policy). — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/duolingos-small-ui-switch-that-changes-everything-fae257203633); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/quitting-duolingo-energy-system-3599842/)

**Robinhood (confetti, free-stock scratch cards)**
- On March 31, 2021, Robinhood stopped showing confetti for customer "firsts" such as deposits, first trades and premium upgrades. It now shows "floating geometric shapes" that CNN called comparatively more sedate. Robinhood called the change an effort to remove a "distraction" from its mission. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-03-31/robinhood-ditches-its-confetti-animation-following-criticism) (snippet); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/31/robinhood-gets-rid-of-confetti-feature-amid-scrutiny-over-gamification.html) (snippet); [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2021/03/31/business/robinhood-confetti-app-changes/index.html) (snippet)
- The 2024 Massachusetts consent order lists the confetti animation, digital scratch tickets and free-stock rewards as game-like features that pushed customers to trade. — [ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices) (snippet); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/01/18/business/robinhood-agrees-pay-75-million-settle-complaints-over-its-sales-practices) (snippet)

**Cleo (AI chat personality, roast/hype)**
- Cleo's assistant has a "hype mode" that praises good saving habits and a "roast mode" that calls out bad spending, ranging "from sarcastic to brutally honest." Sifted reports roast mode is the preferred setting for many of Cleo's roughly 7m users. A team of 15 copywriters and content designers, some of them comedians, writes the lines. — [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/want-to-make-your-ai-chatbot-funny-hiring-comedians-is-a-start) (snippet)

**Long Game (prize-linked savings + casual games)**
- Users earned digital coins for saving and spent them on mini-games with cash prizes. — [Zhang et al. 2021, PLOS ONE (describing Long Game)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8046219); pitched as making "saving money as fun as buying a lottery ticket" — [Fast Company (2016, older)](https://www.fastcompany.com/3065837/the-long-game-wants-to-make-saving-money-as-fun-as-buying-a-lottery-ticket)
- The founder claimed almost 70,000 users adding about $60 a month on average. This is a company claim, not independently verified. — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/9500410) (snippet)
- Truist acquired Long Game in 2022 through Truist Ventures. — [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/40178/truist-buys-savings-gamification-app-long-game) (snippet); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/truist-seeks-to-gamify-savings-with-mobile-app-acquisition) (snippet). A competitor's blog says Truist relaunched it as "Truist Long Game" in 2023 and pulled it from app stores in December 2023. Unverified, from a single competitor source. — [Beem blog](https://trybeem.com/blog/?p=305093) (snippet)

**Qapital, Acorns, Fortune City (secondary roundups)**
- Qapital: users set "rules" and goals that automatically move small amounts into savings as they spend, mixing gamified and goal-based approaches. Acorns: automatically invests "spare change" from everyday purchases (round-ups). Fortune City: logging expenses and income builds a city; a food expense adds a "Food Stall" and income adds a "Cash Register." Coins buy upgrades, and there are achievements and a friends ranking. — [Decode Agency fintech gamification examples](https://decode.agency/article/fintech-app-gamification-examples/); [StriveCloud fintech gamification examples](https://www.strivecloud.io/blog/fintech-gamification-examples) (vendor blogs; snippet-level)

**Chime (SpotMe Boosts)**
- Chime's social mechanic is the SpotMe Boost: a member gives a friend $5 more fee-free overdraft room for the month. Boosts expire monthly, do not come out of the sender's own limit, and recipients "can boost you back." Monthly Boost limits vary by membership tier, and Chime's own docs differ on the exact numbers. — [Chime Help: What are SpotMe Boosts?](https://help.chime.com/what-are-spotme-boosts-738f4c89); [Chime blog (Apr 2, 2021)](https://www.chime.com/blog/spotme-boosts-the-new-way-to-show-your-love/)

**Revolut (RevPoints, challenges, prize draws)**
- Revolut awarded points for completing challenges, such as making transactions, paying another Revolut user, referring friends and setting up Vaults. The points entered weekly in-app prize draws worth £1 to £10,000 (2021). — [Finextra: For Revolut, points means prizes](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/38386/for-revolut-points-means-prizes) (snippet)
- Current RevPoints can be converted to airline miles. Revolut's terms say points have no monetary value except as a reward specifically provides. — [Revolut RevPoints terms (NZ)](https://www.revolut.com/en-NZ/legal/RevPoints/); [Exclusive Access (UK launch)](https://exclusiveaccess.substack.com/p/revpoints-arrive-in-the-uk-what-you) (snippet)
- For comparison, India's CRED runs a daily "spin the wheel" with 10 chances to win bitcoin and vouchers. — [Plotline fintech gamification examples](https://plotline.so/blog/fintech-app-gamification-examples) (snippet)

**Monzo (Year in Monzo / "Wrapped")**
- The 2024 Year in Monzo was a spending recap pinned to the top of the home screen. Users chose a "nice" or "savage" tone, which changed the presentation but not the data. — [Yorkshire Evening Post](https://www.yorkshireeveningpost.co.uk/business/consumer/monzo-wrapped-how-to-find-2024-year-in-review-4911556) (snippet)
- About a Monzo year-end "Wrapped" campaign, Finextra reported an "outpouring of hand-wringing shame and anger." The article page returned 403, so I could not confirm which year's recap it covers or whether the nice/savage toggle existed then. Users disliked being called big spenders at Greggs, Deliveroo, Wetherspoons and KFC, and being reminded of rising prices during the cost-of-living crisis. — [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/43457/monzo-wrapped-prompts-outpouring-of-shame-and-anger) (snippet)
- Guardian coverage (via Freevacy, dated 9 March 2026) says one customer took a complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service after a review mocked her frequent use of food-delivery apps. — [Freevacy summarising The Guardian](https://www.freevacy.com/news/the-guardian/monzo-under-fire-over-misuse-of-personal-data/7195) (snippet)

**Apple Fitness rings**
- watchOS 11 (2024) added "Pause Rings" ("Today," "Until Monday," next month, or a custom date) so users keep their Activity streak during rest or illness. iPhone Life cites a maximum of 90 days. BGR's headline: "With watchOS 11, Pausing My Activity Rings Won't Make Me Feel Bad About Myself Anymore." — [iMore](https://www.imore.com/health-fitness/apple-watch/how-to-pause-activity-streaks-in-watchos-11); [iPhone Life](https://iphonelife.com/content/pause-activity-rings); [BGR](https://bgr.com/tech/with-watchos-11-pausing-my-activity-rings-wont-make-me-feel-bad-about-myself-anymore/)

**Strava (kudos, segments, leaderboards)**
- A master's thesis survey of 94 runners found Strava's gamification raised motivation, which tracked engagement and willingness to pay for premium. Competitive elements were not significantly linked to perceived social pressure. — [Hartsch thesis, Universidade Católica Portuguesa](https://repositorio.ucp.pt/bitstreams/7140c2b8-6f84-4fb7-b300-cd2e4421316a/download) (snippet). A Turku thesis was more cautious: competition can shift runners from enjoying the activity to chasing validation, and some felt pressure. It recommends emphasizing personal progress and inclusivity. — [University of Turku thesis](https://www.utupub.fi/handle/11111/20016) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The best-studied streak systems (Duolingo, Apple) have each made the streak easier to keep: one lesson counts, freezes, pauses. That suggests forgiveness is now standard practice, not a concession.
- The products with backlash or regulatory trouble share a pattern. The game layer rewards the volume of a financial action (trades, transactions, spend) or uses the user's own spending to mock them, and it carries no information that would help the next decision.
- Cleo and Monzo both offer a "kind" and a "harsh" tone. Cleo's roast mode is opt-in banter with a chatbot. Monzo's "savage" recap reached people's real spending and caused complaints. Harsh tone appears to work only when the user explicitly chose it and the content is not identity-shaming.

### Gaps
- No primary source confirmed Acorns-specific milestones/badges, Chime "achievements," or Duolingo leagues' engagement effects. The Duolingo leagues claim above comes only from a user petition.
- I could not confirm whether Monzo apologised or added an opt-out for Year in Monzo. One LinkedIn post claimed it did; no primary or press source confirmed it.
- Long Game's shutdown date rests on a competitor blog.
- No primary Robinhood blog post on the confetti change was retrieved; the details come from press snippets.

## 2. Evidence: does gamification work in personal finance, and for whom?

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses find small-to-moderate positive effects, with heavy heterogeneity. Motivational and behavioral effects are less robust than cognitive ones. In money contexts, the best-controlled evidence (FCA 2024, N=9,140) shows that points, prize draws and notifications reliably raise activity, meaning more transactions. They did not improve decision quality. Saving-focused evidence (prize-linked savings, leaderboards) is positive but limited: it measures intentions, comes from other countries, or relies on press-release figures.

### Cited Findings

**General gamification effectiveness**
- Sailer & Homner's meta-analysis (Educational Psychology Review, 2020) found small, significant effects: cognitive g = .49 (k = 19), motivational g = .36 (k = 16), behavioral g = .25 (k = 9). Only the cognitive effect held up in high-rigor studies; the motivational and behavioral effects were less stable. — [Springer (DOI 10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w) (snippet)
- A 2015–2025 meta-analysis of 28 studies of game-based tools for financial behavior found a positive effect with "substantial heterogeneity." Flow-inducing design and intervention length moderated the effect. I could not confirm journal or effect sizes; this is a 2026 conference/digital-library listing. — [IATED Digital Library](https://library.iated.org/view/LIMADASILVAMORAES2026GAM) (snippet)
- The FCA's own literature review says that "the novelty of such immediate affirmations [celebratory messages, visual confetti] can diminish over time" (citing Rodrigues, 2022). It also notes that most evidence that repeat-visit incentives raise engagement comes from non-financial sectors. — [FCA Research Note, June 2024](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)

**Engagement vs outcomes: the money-app experiments**
- FCA experiment (June 2024; Gathergood, Gilchrist, Hayes, Karapetyan, O'Neill, Stewart; 9,140 completed UK participants, real-money endowment). It tested four features: flashing prices, push notifications, a trader leaderboard, and a points + prize draw (a 70% chance of 1,000 points per buy trade, with more points raising the odds of a £10 prize). Results:
  - Push notifications raised the number of trades by 11% and points + prize draw by 12%. Flashing prices and the leaderboard had no significant effect on trade count.
  - Push notifications and points + prize draw raised the share of trades in risky investments by 8% and 6%. No feature produced a statistically riskier final portfolio.
  - In an exploratory measure (shares bought), push notifications raised purchases by 8% and points + prize draw by 7%. The trader leaderboard raised shares bought by 6%, even though it did not raise the number of trades.
  - People with low financial literacy increased trading more under flashing prices and the leaderboard. Women increased trading more under notifications and the prize draw. People aged 18–34 increased portfolio riskiness more under every feature except flashing prices.
  - The authors say the features "attracted consumer attention, while conveying no additional information which could improve trading." — [FCA Research Note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)
- Prior studies cited in the FCA note:
  - An Ontario Securities Commission (2022) experiment found that points of negligible value (≤$0.08) for trading raised trading activity by 39%.
  - Chapkovski, Khapko & Zoican (2021) found "hedonic gamification" (celebratory messages and achievement badges) linked to 5.2% higher trading volume, and 12.5% higher among people who preferred hedonic design, who also tended to have lower financial literacy.
  - Broihanne (2023) found symbolic badges pushed allocation toward whichever asset they rewarded, risky or safe, while confetti and encouragement messages after trades did not change risk-taking.
  - Moss (2022) found push notifications about ±5% price moves raised Robinhood trading by 25%. — [FCA Research Note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)
- In November 2022 the FCA reported that investors using apps with points, badges and celebratory messages were more likely to invest beyond their risk appetite, and that some showed behavior "similar to problem gambling." The FCA's survey covered 3,000 trading-app users. — [Advisor.ca](https://www.advisor.ca/industry-news/industry/trading-apps-risk-turning-investors-into-gamblers-fca/) (snippet); [FCA Research Note background](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)

**Saving-oriented evidence**
- Prize-linked savings: in South African bank data, people who chose prize-linked savings raised total savings by about 1% of annual income on average. Random prize assignment shows it substitutes for lottery gambling and complements standard saving. (Management Science; South African sample, so generalizability is limited.) — [Cole, Iverson & Tufano, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4047) (snippet); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2441286)
- Leaderboards (Zhang, van Horen & Zeelenberg, PLOS ONE 2021; N = 618 MTurk). A leaderboard raised hypothetical retirement saving (M = 20.74 vs 9.89, ηp² = .06), but only when the comparison standard was high (the leader saved 60% more), not low (10% more). The effect appeared only among regular savers, and lost significance in Study 2 when outliers were included. The authors flag the intention–behavior gap. — [PLOS ONE / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8046219)
- A savings badge product (SavingsQuest) claims 25% higher savings. The figure comes from a company website, not peer review, per Zhang et al. — [PLOS ONE / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8046219)
- Bayes Business School (Scopelliti, Steinmetz, Hashim; Jan 2022) reported that gamified saving apps with badges, leaderboards and progress bars raise goal achievement by "almost 20 per cent." This is a press-release figure; the underlying paper was not retrieved. — [Bayes Business School news](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2022/january/turning-long-term-savings-goals-into-a-game-can-increase-consumer-financial-well-being) (snippet; page returned 403)

**Streak psychology**
- Silverman & Barasch (Journal of Consumer Research, April 2023, 7 studies): showing an intact streak in a behavior log increases later engagement compared with showing a broken one, regardless of actual past behavior. People treat the streak itself as a goal. The drop after a break is larger when people blame themselves, and smaller when they can "repair" the streak. — [CU Boulder Leeds School summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions) (snippet); [UDSpace record](https://udspace.udel.edu/handle/19716/34160)
- Duolingo's own data (above): streak forgiveness (two freezes) increased daily activity rather than reducing it, and lowering the bar to one lesson per day raised retention. — [Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/); [Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)

**Loss framing and avoidance (ostrich effect)**
- An NBER working paper (w23945) uses account-aggregation app data to document "ostrich" avoidance of bad financial information, building on Karlsson et al. (2009, older), which found investors pay less attention to their portfolios after negative returns. — [NBER w23945](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23945.pdf) (snippet)
- A Darden summary notes people avoid checking finances when they suspect bad news. The same authors report (unpublished) that people who don't check show more volatile spending around payday. — [UVA Darden Ideas to Action](https://ideas.darden.virginia.edu/behavior-ostrich-effect) (snippet)
- In a Manulife-based report, 46% of counsellors surveyed said shame makes it hard for people to disclose money problems. — [Wealth Professional](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/news/industry-news/the-vicious-cycle-of-being-financially-unwell/232994) (snippet)

### Inferences
- "Engagement up" and "outcomes up" are separate claims. The rigorous money-app evidence mostly shows the first. A card optimizer can make them coincide, but only if the reward is tied to decision quality (picked the best card) rather than activity (made a purchase, opened the app).
- Broihanne's badge result implies badges are a steering tool: they move behavior toward whatever they reward. That cuts both ways, so badge only behaviors that are good for the user, such as optimizing, learning a category, or adding a card, and never spending volume.
- Loss-framed "missed" totals plausibly work through the same loss aversion Duolingo uses. But the ostrich-effect literature suggests that a large, growing negative number may lead some users to stop opening the app. Loss framing needs a recovery path and gain-framed companions.
- Leaderboards add little in the best evidence (FCA: no effect on trade count; Zhang: intentions only, high standard only, regular savers only) and carry the most social risk.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that tests loss-framed versus gain-framed rewards feedback (e.g., "you missed $X" vs "you captured Y%") in a cashback or card-rewards app.
- No peer-reviewed study of "streak anxiety" as a measured construct in finance apps was found; the evidence is Duolingo's admissions, Silverman & Barasch, and press coverage of Apple's pause feature.
- Hamari et al. (2014) and Koivisto & Hamari (2019) were not retrieved directly; the FCA note cites Hamari (2017).

## 3. Harms, dark patterns, and regulatory scrutiny (US/UK)

### Takeaway
Regulators now treat game-like features in finance as "digital engagement practices" that can cause harm. The concrete remedies target celebrations tied to transaction frequency, chance-based rewards, attention-grabbing notifications, false scarcity, and steering users toward options that pay the platform. US federal rulemaking stalled in 2025, but the 2024 Massachusetts Robinhood order and the UK Consumer Duty remain the clearest concrete standards.

### Cited Findings

**Massachusetts v. Robinhood (2020 complaint, 2024 settlement)**
- On December 16, 2020, the Massachusetts Securities Division filed an administrative complaint (Docket No. E-2020-0047). It alleged Robinhood used strategies "such as gamification to encourage and entice continuous and repetitive use of its trading application." It also alleged one investor with no experience made over 12,700 trades in six months, and that about 68% of Massachusetts customers approved for options had limited or no investment experience. The complaint was the first action under the state's fiduciary rule. — [Galvin press release PDF via NAPA](https://www.napa-net.org/sites/napa-net.org/files/2020.12.16%20Galvin%20Charges%20Robinhood%20over%20Gamification.pdf) (snippet); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswz35tktbxpyerdh2io/culture/robinhood-allegedly-lures-inexperienced-investors-fails-to-properly-screen-for-options-trading-massachusetts-complaint-says) (snippet); [MSD Amended Complaint](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/securities/download/MSD-Robinhood-Amended-Complaint-Docket%20No-%20E-2020-0047.pdf)
- In January 2024 Robinhood paid $7.5M to settle. For Massachusetts accounts, as summarized in press coverage (the consent order itself was not retrieved), it must stop celebratory imagery tied to the frequency of trading, push notifications highlighting specific lists, and features that mimic games of chance. It must also add disclosures to its lists and hire an independent compliance consultant. Robinhood neither admitted nor denied the gamification allegations and rejected the premise that its app was ever gamified. A company lawyer said the settlement resolves historical matters from 2021 that do not reflect Robinhood today (paraphrased from press coverage). — [ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices) (snippet); [InvestmentNews](https://investmentnews.com/regulation-and-legislation/news/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-to-settle-massachusetts-charges-248214) (snippet); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/01/18/business/robinhood-agrees-pay-75-million-settle-complaints-over-its-sales-practices) (snippet)

**SEC: digital engagement practices**
- On August 27, 2021 (Press Release 2021-167), the SEC requested comment on "digital engagement practices." The release lists "behavioral prompts," "differential marketing," "game-like features (commonly referred to as gamification)," and "other design elements or features designed to engage with retail investors on digital platforms." Gensler said "these features may encourage investors to trade more often, invest in different products" and, separately, "This may lead to conflicts between the platform and investors." — [SEC Press Release 2021-167](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2021-167)
- File No. S7-10-21. The request's DEP categories include social networking tools, games and contests with prizes, notifications and chatbots, per SIFMA's October 1, 2021 comment letter. — [SIFMA comment letter](https://www.sifma.org/advocacy/letters/sec-request-for-information-and-comment-on-digital-engagement-practices) (snippet)
- In August 2023 the SEC proposed a "predictive data analytics" conflicts rule requiring firms to "eliminate or neutralize" conflicts from such technologies. It withdrew the proposal on June 12, 2025, with 13 others, and said any future action would need a new proposal. A bipartisan Senate group had urged withdrawal. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/sec-formally-withdraws-fourteen-rule-proposals) (snippet); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/06/sec-clears-unfinished-rulemakings-from-regulatory-agenda) (snippet); [Sen. Rounds press release](https://www.rounds.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/rounds-heinrich-crapo-and-warner-direct-sec-to-withdraw-predictive-data-analytics-rule) (snippet)

**FTC: "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (Sept 15, 2022)**
- The staff report followed an April 2021 workshop and covers websites, mobile apps and VR/AR. Its categories include designs that create false beliefs (false scarcity claims, countdown timers, comparison sites that claim neutrality while selling placement, ads disguised as content), designs leading to unauthorized charges, obstructive designs, and manipulation of data-sharing choices. — [Hunton Privacy Blog](https://www.huntonprivacyblog.com/2022/09/27/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns/) (snippet); [Orrick InfoBytes](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2022-09-23/ftc-reports-use-dark-patterns/) (snippet)

**CFPB: steering by comparison tools (directly relevant to a card picker)**
- In February 2024 the CFPB issued Circular 2024-01. It warned that comparison-shopping tools for credit cards, loans and bank accounts can break the law when they steer consumers to products because of lender kickbacks, including through dark patterns. — [CFPB newsroom (archived)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-issues-guidance-to-rein-in-rigged-comparison-shopping-results-for-credit-cards-and-other-financial-products/)
- In May 2025 the CFPB rescinded about 67 guidance documents, including Circular 2024-01. The underlying UDAAP authority remains; the circular as guidance does not. — [Consumer Finance Monitor (Ballard Spahr)](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/05/16/cfpb-rescinds-67-guidance-documents/) (snippet); [Morgan Lewis CFPB Guidance Tracker](https://www.morganlewis.com/topics/cfpb-guidance-tracker-rescinded-and-remaining) (snippet; sources disagree on a separate 2023 mortgage advisory opinion)

**UK FCA: Consumer Duty and gamification**
- The Consumer Duty came into force on 31 July 2023 for products on sale. The FCA's 2024 experiment was framed explicitly "in light of" the Duty and concludes that "firms and regulators should continue to closely scrutinise the effect of trading app design features on consumer investment decisions." — [FCA Research Note, June 2024](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)
- Practices the FCA flagged in 2022: using and commodifying points and badges for trades, very frequent market-news notifications, and leaderboards ranking trading performance. — [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102i2xb/fca-publishes-research-on-gamification-of-trading-apps) (snippet); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/regulation/trading-apps-risk-turning-investors-into-gamblers-fca/) (snippet)
- Kroll's reading of FCA expectations: use gamification "only where it supports the delivery of good customer outcomes," avoid foreseeable harm, and support "effective, timely and properly informed decisions." — [Kroll](https://www.kroll.com/en/publications/financial-compliance-regulation/positive-consumer-outcomes-gamified-financial-services) (snippet); [FStech](https://www.fstech.co.uk/fst/FCA_Raises_Concerns_About_Gamification_Of_Trading_Apps.php) (snippet)

**Trust failures in savings/coupon tools**
- PayPal Honey (December 2024): a MegaLag investigation alleged Honey replaced creators' affiliate tags with its own even when it found no coupon, and favored partner-approved coupons over better ones. PayPal said it followed standard last-click attribution. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/23/paypal-honey-browser-extension-controversy/) (snippet); [Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/honey-affiliate-backlash) (snippet)

- Monzo's shaming recap (Section 1) reached the Financial Ombudsman. — [Freevacy/Guardian](https://www.freevacy.com/news/the-guardian/monzo-under-fire-over-misuse-of-personal-data/7195) (snippet)

**Streak anxiety (product-side admissions)**
- Duolingo acknowledges that fear of losing a streak can make people avoid a challenge altogether. — [Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/). Apple's Pause Rings was received as relief from feeling bad about breaking a streak. — [BGR](https://bgr.com/tech/with-watchos-11-pausing-my-activity-rings-wont-make-me-feel-bad-about-myself-anymore/). Silverman & Barasch show a broken streak lowers later engagement, especially when users blame themselves. — [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)

### Inferences
- For a simulated demo, none of these rules apply legally: no real money, not a broker-dealer, no real affiliate revenue. But they work as a practical checklist for what reads as "manipulative" to judges and users. If CardMax ever earned issuer or affiliate fees, the CFPB 2024-01 steering concern (even though the guidance is rescinded) and the Honey backlash describe exactly the trust failure a card optimizer must avoid. The recommended card must be the one that is best for the user, and the app should say so.
- The Massachusetts remedies amount to a concrete design spec: no celebration tied to how often you transact, no chance-based mechanics, and disclosure on any curated list. That spec is easy for CardMax to follow because its core value is per-decision quality, not transaction volume.
- "Daily deals" is where CardMax sits closest to FTC dark-pattern territory: countdown timers, false scarcity, ads disguised as editorial picks.

- I did not retrieve the FTC report PDF or the Massachusetts consent order itself; the remedy wording comes from press coverage.
- No FCA guidance document specific to gamification outside trading apps (e.g., banking or cashback) was found. The Consumer Duty application to gamification here comes from FCA research notes and law-firm/consultancy commentary.
- The status of the PayPal Honey lawsuits was reported inconsistently in search snippets and is not included.

## 4. Design guidance: playful but not manipulative; framing "missed money" without shame

### Takeaway
The evidence points to a consistent rule: reward the quality of a decision the user was already making, make every streak or penalty forgivable, give celebrations information, scale them to the outcome, and use them sparingly. Never put chance-based rewards, leaderboards or timers next to spending. Frame "missed" money as recoverable and forward-looking, with a tone the user chooses.

### Cited Findings
- Features that "convey no additional information which could improve" decisions still raised activity (FCA). The FCA treats that as a harm signal, not a success metric. — [FCA Research Note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)
- Chance-based rewards linked to activity (points + prize draw) increased transactions by 12% and the share of risky trades by 6%. Massachusetts barred features that mimic games of chance (press-coverage wording). — [FCA Research Note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf); [ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices) (snippet)
- Celebrations: Massachusetts barred celebratory imagery tied to the frequency of trading (press-coverage wording). Robinhood swapped confetti for calmer geometric shapes. Celebratory novelty fades over time. — [ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices) (snippet); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-03-31/robinhood-ditches-its-confetti-animation-following-criticism) (snippet); [FCA Research Note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)
- Forgiveness raises engagement: two equipped freezes gave +0.38% DAU, and a one-lesson streak gave +3.3% D14 retention. Repairable streaks soften the drop after a break, and Apple added Pause Rings. — [Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/); [Duolingo Blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak); [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); [iMore](https://www.imore.com/health-fitness/apple-watch/how-to-pause-activity-streaks-in-watchos-11)
- Reward over punishment: Duolingo framed hearts-to-energy as rewarding correct answers instead of punishing mistakes, though it still drew backlash over monetization. — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/duolingos-small-ui-switch-that-changes-everything-fae257203633)
- Tone choice: Cleo's roast is opt-in, and Monzo's 2024 recap let users pick "nice" or "savage." Monzo's year-end recaps still drew shame and an ombudsman complaint by naming specific everyday purchases. I couldn't confirm which recap year each complaint concerns. — [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/want-to-make-your-ai-chatbot-funny-hiring-comedians-is-a-start) (snippet); [Yorkshire Evening Post](https://www.yorkshireeveningpost.co.uk/business/consumer/monzo-wrapped-how-to-find-2024-year-in-review-4911556) (snippet); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/43457/monzo-wrapped-prompts-outpouring-of-shame-and-anger) (snippet)
- Vulnerable groups react more: people with low financial literacy, younger users and, for some features, women showed larger effects. Hedonic gamification raised trading most among those who liked it, who also had lower literacy. — [FCA Research Note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)
- Avoidance: people avoid checking finances when they expect bad news, and shame inhibits disclosure of money problems. — [Darden](https://ideas.darden.virginia.edu/behavior-ostrich-effect) (snippet); [Wealth Professional](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/news/industry-news/the-vicious-cycle-of-being-financially-unwell/232994) (snippet)
- Leaderboards work only with a high, plausible standard, only for people already saving, and only on intentions. — [PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8046219)

### Inferences
Practical rules derived from the findings:
1. **Reward decision quality, not volume.** XP and streaks count "best card used" purchases out of purchases made, never purchase count or dollars spent. Never prompt "make a purchase to keep your streak."
2. **Celebrate with information, and scale it.** "Sapphire earned 3x here: +$2.40 vs your default card" beats confetti. Save the big moment for milestones such as a first coupon stack or a month at 100%, and make it a one-off rather than a repeat on every purchase. Ship a reduced-motion variant.
3. **Forgive by default.** Offer a streak freeze or "repair" (for example, the next optimal purchase restores it), or define the streak by week so a missed day doesn't break it.
4. **No chance mechanics near money.** No spin wheels, scratch cards or loot boxes tied to purchases. If anything is random, keep it cosmetic and unrelated to spending.
5. **Frame "missed" as "recoverable."** Show a gain-framed capture rate next to the missed total, attach a one-tap fix ("Set Amex Gold as default for dining"), and describe the cause neutrally ("the card used didn't earn the dining bonus"), not as a personal failing. Avoid naming merchants mockingly. Any roast tone is opt-in.
6. **Opt-out and quiet mode.** Let users hide the game layer (streaks, badges, celebrations) and keep the plain utility. This addresses streak anxiety and the people who react most to these features.
7. **Disclose ranking logic** on deals and recommendations, because steering is the core trust risk for a comparison tool.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found that tests specific copy for "missed money" messaging. The rules in point 5 are inferences from loss-aversion, ostrich-effect and backlash evidence, not direct tests.
- No source quantified opt-out rates or the effects of a "hide gamification" toggle.

## 5. Which 3–6 mechanics fit a card-rewards optimizer, and how comparable rewards apps present savings

### Takeaway
Direct comparables (Kudos, MaxRewards, CardPointers) present savings as utility (dashboards, perk trackers, "missed perks" recovery) rather than games. Receipt and cashback apps (Fetch) lean on variable rewards and have taken user backlash when they change them. A card optimizer has the rare property that "playing well" and "the user's best interest" are the same thing: choosing the optimal card. That makes decision-quality gamification defensible, as long as it never rewards spending more.

### Cited Findings

**Card optimizers**
- Kudos works as a checkout-time wallet. It recommends the best card and stacks cash back, with a free tier and Premium at about $71.95/year. Source is Kudos' own comparison, with an affiliate disclosure. — [Kudos blog: Kudos vs CardPointers](https://www.joinkudos.com/blog/kudos-vs-cardpointers-which-app-maximizes-your-rewards-better-2025) (snippet)
- Kudos' App Store listing promises to "take back $300 in missed credit card perks every year" and shows a benefits tracker and rewards dashboard. A Kudos blog post is titled "The $200 Billion Credit Card Secret: How Americans Leave Billions on the Table." That is loss-framed marketing. — [App Store: Kudos](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kudos-put-your-wallet-to-work/id6443548838) (snippet); [Kudos blog](https://www.joinkudos.com/blog/the-200-billion-credit-card-secret-how-americans-leave-billions-on-the-table)
- MaxRewards is a card-management app for 1,000+ cards. It offers reward and benefit tracking, a spending dashboard with reward-earning breakdowns, and help activating offers. Gold costs about $84–$108/year depending on source and date. — [College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/46251/kudos-review/) (snippet); [CardStack comparison](https://cardstack.money/articles/guides/maxrewards-vs-kudos) (snippet); [Kudos' MaxRewards review (competitor-written)](https://www.joinkudos.com/blog/maxrewards-credit-card-app-review) (snippet)
- CardPointers is a tool you check before paying. It supports 5,000+ cards and costs about $72/year. 9to5Mac (2022) called it an excellent app from indie developer Emmanuel Crouvisier. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/09/06/best-credit-cards-rewards-app-iphone/) (snippet); [Kudos blog](https://www.joinkudos.com/blog/kudos-vs-cardpointers-which-app-maximizes-your-rewards-better-2025) (snippet)

**Cashback/receipt apps**
- Fetch: a "Daily Reward" wheel, one spin per 24 hours after scanning a receipt, which its blog suggested setting a daily reminder for. — [Fetch blog](https://fetch.com/blog/fetch-tips-tricks/everything-you-need-to-know-about-fetchs-10k-daily-reward-wheel-including-how-to-hack-it). In January 2024 Fetch replaced it with "Fetch Play" (games for points). An executive said the average user earns 3,500 points per Fetch Play session versus 5–25 from the daily reward. Users complained ("Bring back the daily spin"). — [Coupons in the News](https://couponsinthenews.com/2024/01/08/fetch-introduces-games-for-points-pleasing-some-and-irking-others/) (snippet)
- Ibotta: no spin or streak mechanics found. A comparison describes choosing offers before buying and then scanning the receipt, which is a transactional model. — [Michael Saves comparison](https://michaelsaves.com/save-money/fetch-rewards-vs-ibotta-comparison) (snippet)
- Honey: a savings tool whose trust collapsed over alleged undisclosed affiliate capture and coupon preferencing (Section 3). — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/23/paypal-honey-browser-extension-controversy/) (snippet)

### Inferences
**Recommended shortlist for CardMax (each tied to evidence above):**
1. **"Maximized" streak, counted per purchase, with a built-in freeze or repair.** The streak continues each time the user pays with the recommended card. A suboptimal purchase spends a freeze or can be repaired by the next optimal one; there is no daily-login requirement. Evidence: Duolingo's one-lesson change and freezes, Silverman & Barasch on repair, Apple Pause Rings. It rewards decision quality, not volume (FCA, Massachusetts).
2. **Capture-rate ring or meter** ("You captured 94% of available rewards this month"), shown as an Apple-style ring next to the "Cash back missed" counter. Pair the missed figure with a gain-framed percentage and a one-tap fix. Evidence: loss aversion (Duolingo), ostrich effect (NBER/Darden), Monzo backlash, Apple's ring metaphor, and weakly the Bayes progress-bar claim (press-release figure only).
3. **Small celebration that carries information when a purchase is maximized.** Show the dollar delta against the default card, use a short animation and haptic, and reserve a bigger moment for real milestones. Keep everything static under reduced motion. Evidence: the Massachusetts remedy, Robinhood's switch to calmer visuals, novelty fade (FCA/Rodrigues), Chapkovski hedonic effects.
4. **Skill badges for learning and setup, not for spending.** Examples: "Dining pro: used the best dining card 10 times," "First triple-stack (card + coupon + deal)," "Wallet complete." Evidence: Broihanne shows badges steer whatever they reward, so reward optimization; the 2022 FCA concern targeted points and badges for trades.
5. **Daily deals as optional quests with no timers or scarcity, plus a short "why this card/deal" disclosure.** Evidence: FTC false-scarcity and countdown categories, CFPB 2024-01 steering concerns, Honey's coupon-preferencing allegation, the Massachusetts list-disclosure remedy.
6. **(Optional) Tone toggle for the missed-cash-back copy**, defaulting to "kind," with an opt-in "roast." Evidence: Cleo's opt-in roast is popular; Monzo's "savage" recap shamed people over real purchases.

**Avoid:** spending-based leaderboards (FCA found no trade-count effect, larger effects among low-literacy users; Zhang found effects only under narrow conditions), spin wheels and scratch cards tied to purchases (FCA prize-draw +12%, Massachusetts games-of-chance ban, Fetch's wheel), XP per dollar spent, and push notifications urging purchases (FCA notifications +11% trades).

**Gap in the market:** none of the three direct card-optimizer comparables showed visible gamification in the retrieved sources. A tasteful, decision-quality game layer would set CardMax apart rather than copy competitors. Kudos' loss-framed "missed perks" marketing suggests the "missed" metric is a recognized hook.

- No primary product documentation was retrieved for in-app "missed rewards" features at MaxRewards or CardPointers; their presence or absence is unconfirmed.
- Pricing figures for card optimizers come from competitor-written or dated reviews.

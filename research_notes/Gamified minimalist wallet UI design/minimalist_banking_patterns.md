# Minimalist banking, payments and personal-finance app design patterns

Scope note: these notes cover Cash App, Revolut, Monzo, Copilot Money, Robinhood, Apple Card (Wallet), Chime, Nubank and Mercury, plus NN/g guidance. Every dated source is marked inline. Several of the best primary sources are older (2006, 2015, 2019, 2022, 2023), and the notes flag them. Apple HIG and material.io pages could not be fetched because they render with JavaScript, so platform-guideline claims rest on NN/g instead, plus Material Components' GitHub docs for dark theme. Rules written specifically for CardMax are synthesis and appear only under **Inferences**.

## 1. Home-screen structure, hero number, transaction rows, and secondary actions in the leading apps

### Takeaway
The redesigned homes that are documented (Monzo 2022–23, Revolut 10 in 2023, Mercury, Apple Card in Wallet) share an order: the user's financial state comes first (one balance or account widget), then a short list of recent activity, and controls and secondary products sit one tap down. Monzo's team defines simplicity as "bringing order to complexity", not removing things. The apps keep the core jobs (see balance, find a transaction, get card details) on the surface and move everything else behind a tap, a menu or a widget the user chooses.

### Cited Findings
**Monzo**
- Monzo's Nov 2023 home-screen rebuild took "over a year", reached "more than 8 million people", involved "4 major and 2 minor experiments over the last 7 months" and more than 1,000 customers. The team's stated definition: "Simplicity isn't the absence of things, it's bringing order to complexity." — [Monzo blog, 6 Nov 2023](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen)
- The core jobs the home screen had to keep easy were viewing balances, copying card details and searching for transactions. The app had "outgrown its own design", so customers struggled to discover features. — [Monzo blog, 2023](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen)

- Monzo did not interrupt customers "when they enter the app to do a task" with the redesign. Users could snooze the upgrade. — [Monzo blog, 2023](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen)

### Inferences
- **The common home skeleton (synthesis):** (1) one hero object, either a balance or a single account/card widget; (2) one to three primary actions attached to it; (3) a short recent-activity list; (4) everything else one tap away, through widgets, an "add product" menu or a second tab. Monzo, Revolut 10, Mercury and Apple Card all fit this. None of the documented redesigns puts explanations, multiple stat blocks or promotional tiles above the activity list.
- **Customisation as decluttering:** Monzo (user-controlled home) and Revolut and Robinhood (widgets) handle feature sprawl by letting users choose what appears. For a four-tab hackathon app this matters less than a strict fixed hierarchy.
- **The detail view carries the explanation:** Apple Card keeps rows terse and puts category, Daily Cash, map, merchant history and monthly total in the transaction detail. This matches CardMax's "why this card won": put it behind a tap, not inline.
- **CardMax Wallet rule:** the best card's face is the hero. Under it goes one line with the single number that matters ("Earns $3.20 here" or "4× at Whole Foods") and one primary action (hold to pay). Put "Why this card" in a sheet opened by tapping the card or a small text link, not in an always-visible bordered box.
- **CardMax Missed rule:** one hero number (total missed, large) with at most a short period label, then the bar chart, then the list. This mirrors Apple Card's period totals followed by transactions.

## 2. How rewards and cash back are presented (Apple Daily Cash, Cash App Boosts, Revolut RevPoints, Robinhood Gold Card)

### Takeaway
The rewards designs with the least friction express rewards as **money attached to the transaction**: Apple's Daily Cash appears per transaction and as weekly, monthly and yearly totals. Points-based systems (RevPoints, Robinhood Gold's points) bury rewards in a separate section or tab and generate confusion about multipliers and value. Cash App Boosts keeps the choice tiny: one active offer at a time, activated before purchase.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple Card Daily Cash:** each transaction's detail shows the Daily Cash earned on it next to the charge amount and a map. Weekly, Monthly and Yearly Activity screens show total Daily Cash per period, with swipe-back through earlier periods. — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT209227)
- Apple's activity view breaks spending down by category and "the amount of Daily Cash that you've earned back". Daily Cash was deposited daily into Apple Cash (2019 description). — [MacStories, 2019](https://www.macstories.net/news/apple-card-the-macstories-overview/)
- Launch-era Daily Cash: "Most transactions pay 1%", "buying anything from Apple bumps that up to 3%", and Apple Pay purchases "offer twice as much Daily Cash back" as the physical card (that is, 2%). This is 2019 information, and the current rates were not checked. — [Cult of Mac, 14 Aug 2019](https://www.cultofmac.com/reviews/apple-card-review)
- **Cash App Boosts:** reached from the Cash Card icon on home through "Save With Boost". Only one Boost can be active at a time, and it must be activated before the purchase. It applies only when paying with the Cash Card at eligible merchants. — [CreditCards.com](https://www.creditcards.com/card-advice/cash-app-boost/). A claimed rename from "Boosts" to "Offers" appeared only on a low-credibility site and is unconfirmed.
- **Revolut RevPoints:** balance and earning history sit in a dedicated RevPoints area under the Lifestyle section on Home. — [Revolut Help](https://help.revolut.com/en-MT/help/app-features/what-are-revpoints/); [RevOptimize guide (third-party)](https://revoptimize.fr/en/revpoints/). Earning rate depends on plan: 1 point per €10 spent on Standard, 1 per €1 on Ultra. — [Bonkers.ie](https://www.bonkers.ie/blog/credit-cards/revolut-expands-its-loyalty-rewards-programme-to-its-credit-card-customers/). Anecdotal: users report a "0" points counter top right when none are held — [Head for Points forum](https://www.headforpoints.com/forums/topic/revpoints-2/page/2) — and Reddit users found the switch from "×" multiplier wording to "per" wording confusing (via a Reddit mirror; anecdotal).
- **Robinhood Gold Card:** marketed at launch (Mar 2024) as 3% cash back on everything and 5% on travel through its portal. — [Crowdfund Insider, Mar 2024](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2024/03/223195-robinhood-introduces-new-gold-credit-card/). Robinhood's support page says it earns **3× points** by default and up to 5× through the travel portal. The points are worth 1¢ each, so "3% cash back" simplifies how it actually works. — [Robinhood Support](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-gold-card-rewards); [Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/robinhood-gold-card-not-really-3-percent-cash-back/)
- Robinhood's rewards path is Banking app → Credit Card → Rewards, which shows the point balance and 4 redemption options. Redemptions appear in the home-screen transaction history. Cash back lands in the investing app as "Cash back reward from Robinhood credit card". Without Robinhood Banking, Rewards is its own tab. — [Robinhood Support](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-gold-card-rewards)
- **Monzo Flex** design principle: "never being more than one-click away from a purchase breakdown." — [Monzo blog, Nov 2022](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)

### Inferences
- **Show rewards in dollars, not points or multipliers.** Apple Daily Cash (a dollar amount on each transaction) is the clearest model. The Revolut multiplier confusion and Robinhood's 3× points versus "3% cash back" gap show what happens otherwise. For CardMax, every reward value should read as "$X back". The rate (4×, 3%) can appear as a quiet secondary token, but the dollar figure is the hero.
- **Attach the reward to the row; aggregate it in the period view.** Apple uses two levels: per-transaction Daily Cash in detail, and per-period totals in Activity. CardMax Missed maps onto this directly: rows show "$1.80 missed" and the hero shows the period total.
- **One choice at a time.** Cash App's one-active-Boost rule cuts decision cost (Hick's law, which Monzo cites). For CardMax Scan, show one best coupon and one best card with one total, and put alternatives behind "Other options".
- **Comparison presentation (synthesis):** to compare the used card with the best card in Missed, one line per purchase is enough: merchant, "Used Amex Gold · Best: Citi Double", amount missed right-aligned. A side-by-side box per purchase is not needed. No source showed a used-vs-best comparison UI directly, so this is unsourced design reasoning.

### Gaps
- No source showed how Revolut displays RevPoints on each transaction row, or whether Chime shows any rewards (for example, SpotMe or other Chime features).
- No current (2025–26) screenshots or official description of Apple Card's main Wallet screen were fetched. The 2019 MacStories and launch reviews may be outdated.
- No source on Robinhood Gold Card rewards screen visuals.

## 3. Navigation conventions: tab bars, FABs, bottom sheets vs full-screen pushes

### Takeaway
Use a persistent, labelled bottom tab bar of at most 5 destinations (4 is fine). Reserve bottom sheets for quick, context-preserving detail or actions: never stacked, always with a visible close button, and never as a replacement for page-to-page flows. Keep the disclosure hierarchy to two levels. No sourced guidance on FABs in finance apps was found.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g (2015, older but still the standard primer): "If your site has more than 5 options, it's hard to fit them in a tab or navigation bar" at good touch-target sizes. Labelling icons is "a recommended best practice in most cases". Tab bars stay visible "whether the user scrolls down the page or not". Scrollable tab bars hide options users may never find. — [NN/g, Raluca Budiu, Nov 2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-navigation-patterns/)
- NN/g's core navigation principle is to "prioritize content over chrome". When 4–5 main options are needed often, show them all. Hamburger menus are less discoverable, since "many people still simply don't think to open it". — [NN/g, 2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-navigation-patterns/)
- **Bottom sheets (NN/g, Jun 2023):** a sheet is a form of progressive disclosure for information "not always needed". It keeps part of the current context visible. Modal sheets block the background; nonmodal sheets don't. Expandable sheets "typically" start nonmodal and become modal when expanded. — [NN/g, Page Laubheimer, Jun 2023](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)
- NN/g bottom-sheet rules: use sheets when users "need to refer to the main, background information". Avoid them for always-needed information, for "typical page-to-page user flows" and for "complex content". Provide "a clear Close button" because the grab handle is easy to miss and conflicts with system gestures. Support the Back gesture, especially for full-screen sheets. **Avoid stacking sheets**, which NN/g calls one of the biggest problems. — [NN/g, 2023](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)
- **Progressive disclosure (NN/g, 2006, foundational):** show "only a few of the most important options" first. Designs beyond two levels "typically have low usability". Label the link to the next level clearly ("information scent"). Use staged disclosure (wizard-like steps) only when steps have little interdependence. — [NN/g, Jakob Nielsen, Dec 2006](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)
- Revolut 10 puts secondary product areas "in just one tap" from home rather than in a menu. — [Revolut, 2023](https://www.revolut.com/news/revolut_launches_revolut_10_as_it_targets_primary_accounts_and_passes_35m_customers_worldwide)
- Block's Cash App "Core Experience" design team owns "the app's structure, from navigation to how features relate to each other". — [Block job posting via Built In](https://builtin.com/job/principal-product-designer-core-experience/7337904)

### Inferences
- **CardMax's 4 tabs (Wallet, Scan, Deals, Missed) fit the ≤5 rule.** Keep icons with short one-word labels, filled when active and outline when inactive. No badges unless something truly needs attention; a Missed count badge would add anxiety.
- **Sheet versus push for CardMax:** "Why this card won" fits a nonmodal or half-height sheet (quick, refers back to the card). The Scan result to "Which card did you pay with?" step is staged disclosure (two short steps) and fits a push or an in-place step change. Avoid opening a sheet from a sheet, for example a deal detail sheet that opens a card sheet.
- **No FAB:** Scan already has its own tab, so a FAB would duplicate it. This is unsourced reasoning.

### Gaps
- Apple HIG (tab bars, sheets, Liquid Glass in iOS 26) and Material 3 (navigation bar, FAB) pages could not be fetched (JavaScript-rendered). Their exact current guidance on tab counts, FABs and sheet detents is unverified here.
- No source on which of these finance apps use FABs or how many tabs each currently has (for example, Revolut's or Monzo's exact 2025 tab set).

## 4. Visual system: typography scale, colour, dark surfaces, radii, spacing, iconography

### Takeaway
The leading brands use a **neutral base plus one signature accent** (Robinhood's "Robin Neon" on black, white and neutrals; Cash App's fluorescent green; Mercury's calm green with red and yellow reserved for errors and warnings). Each pairs it with a custom or near-custom typeface and a disciplined icon set. NN/g's minimalism heuristic says not to "overuse font or color variation". Money figures should use tabular numerals so amounts align and live-updating numbers don't jitter. No primary source on radii, spacing scales or exact dark-surface values was retrievable. Material's Android docs give only qualitative rules: dark grey rather than black, and lighter surfaces at higher elevation.

### Cited Findings
- **Robinhood (Porto Rocha refresh, 2024, "fully integrated across all surfaces by early 2025"):** the core insight was "when it comes to standing out in a sea of fintech sameness, less is more." The base palette is black, white and "mature neutrals", with one accent, "Robin Neon", an electric yellow-green "used in pops", contrasted with competitors' rainbow palettes. The type is RH Phonic (sans, primary text; "delicate ink-traps bring personality without sacrificing precision") plus Martina Plantijn (serif, headlines). Illustration is "inspired by financial graphs" and "easy to replicate and scale". — [Porto Rocha case study](https://www.portorocha.com/robinhood)
- Robinhood's earlier 2020 identity (with COLLINS) used Capsule Sans and Nib serif. — [Robinhood newsroom, Aug 2020](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/a-visual-identity-that-better-reflects-our-vision/). Auto-generated "style guides" online conflict on Robinhood's current type stack and were excluded.
- **Cash App (fall 2025):** brand green made "more vibrant fluorescent green". New proprietary typeface "Cash Sans". "Over 650 new icons". "Custom motion and responsive feedback" throughout. 3D graphic elements, a "sophisticated grid system", and personalised visual assets on payment and card screens. Framed as a "recalibration" of personality, not a redesign. — [Design Compass, Dec 2025](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/12/03/cash-apps-new-design-language-and-ai-moneybot/); [Nasdaq press release, Nov 2025](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/introducing-cash-app-releases-see-whats-new-2025-11-13)
- **Mercury (third-party analysis):** the palette is "built around calm, not energy". Green is primary, blue secondary, and red and yellow appear **only** for errors and warnings. Functional and decorative colour are kept separate (OKLCH scale). Mercury reportedly avoids red next to negative balances or bills, calling that a source of anxiety, and avoids "promotional oranges" and "engagement-bait purples". — [925Studios, 2026](https://www.925studios.co/blog/mercury-design-breakdown)
- Mercury's design job postings stress "typography, spacing, color, and motion" as craft priorities. Its brand copy argues that "care and craft in design has been lost in modern finance". — [Working Nomads: Mercury Senior Product Designer](https://www.workingnomads.com/jobs/senior-product-designer-mercury); [Mercury Senior Brand Designer](https://www.workingnomads.com/jobs/senior-brand-designer-expressive-ui-mercury)
- **Apple Card:** each spending category has an assigned colour, used to spot category membership quickly (2019). — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/apple-card-the-macstories-overview/)
- **NN/g heuristic #8:** "Don't overuse font or color variation". "Avoid multipurpose visual cues", such as styling unclickable text like links. Give every element a purpose, including negative space. Users form aesthetic impressions in about 50 ms. — [NN/g, Therese Fessenden, Jan 2021](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-minimalist-design/)
- **Tabular numerals:** `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` makes digits fixed-width with equal spacing (OpenType `tnum`). — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-numeric). Tailwind exposes a `tabular-nums` utility and warns that "many fonts don't support these features". — [Tailwind docs](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/font-variant-numeric). Dialpad's design system recommends it for aligning tabular data and "preventing layout shifts of dynamically-changing content". — [Dialtone](https://dialtone.dialpad.com/utilities/typography/font-variant-numeric.html)
- **Motion on money:** Monzo planned a "balance ticker animation when your balance updates". — [Monzo Community, Dec 2022](https://community.monzo.com/t/app-evolution-now-in-monzo-labs/141702). Monzo's card-freeze uses a frost animation to show state. — [Monzo blog, 2022](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)
- **Design systems as consistency enforcers:** Nubank's NuDS is built in Figma with tokens and theming across 118M+ customers in three countries. A Figma plugin flags outdated components before handoff. — [Figma customer story (vendor-published)](https://www.figma.com/customers/nubank-design-system-accessible-experiences-with-figma/). Nubank: "As Nu's offerings continue to expand, ensuring consistency, predictability, and usability becomes paramount. NuDS addresses this challenge head-on, reducing the cognitive load on our customers". — [Building Nu, 30 Oct 2023](https://building.nu.com/tailoring-products-for-fanatical-customers-in-various-countries/)
- **Dark surfaces (Material Components for Android docs; the canonical material.io spec could not be fetched):** Material's baseline dark theme uses "dark grey instead of black" for background and surface, because dark grey "increases visibility for shadows" and "reduces eye strain for light text". Elevation is shown by making higher surfaces lighter, with semi-transparent primary-colour overlays whose alpha rises with elevation. The docs give no exact percentages. The dark theme also ships adjusted defaults for the primary, secondary and tertiary colours. — [material-components-android, Dark.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Dark.md)

### Inferences
- **One accent, semantic colours rationed (synthesis of Robinhood, Mercury and Cash App):** CardMax should pick one brand accent for the primary action and the "best" state, and use green only for money gained. Red should appear sparingly or not at all. For Missed, a muted amber or the neutral text colour may be less punishing than red, following Mercury's anti-anxiety stance.
- **Typography budget:** two weights (regular and semibold) and about four sizes (hero number, title, body, caption) fit NN/g's "don't overuse font variation" and the custom-type discipline in these brands. This is synthesis; no source gives an exact scale.
- **Money typography:** tabular-nums on every amount, especially lists, counters and the Missed ticker. De-emphasise cents in hero numbers (smaller or lower contrast), which is unverified as a sourced pattern (see Gaps). Right-align amounts in rows.
- **Shared tokens fix the "each screen built by a different person" problem:** the Nubank and Monzo approach (one design language rolled out tab by tab, enforced by tokens) applies directly. Define surface, text, accent, radius and spacing tokens once in Tailwind config and ban raw hex values in screens.

### Gaps
- **Exact dark-mode surface values** (for example the commonly cited #121212 base, specific overlay percentages, desaturated accents, text-opacity tiers): the material.io spec pages failed to fetch (JS-rendered). Only the qualitative rules above (dark grey, not black; lighter surfaces at higher elevation) are verified. No finance app's dark palette was sourced. Apple's convention, a pure-black base in iOS dark mode, was not verified either.
- **Corner radii, spacing rhythm (4 or 8 pt grids), icon stroke weights:** no primary or reliable secondary source found for any of the target apps.
- **"Smaller cents" balance pattern (Monzo, Revolut, Cash App):** commonly seen but unsourced in this research.
- Exact hex values for Robin Neon and Cash App green were not published in the sources found.

## 5. Principles stated by the design teams themselves

### Takeaway
Monzo is the only target company with substantial first-party design writing. Its principles: predictability before delight, ration attention ("if everything is a highlight then nothing is"), Hick's law, unambiguous affordance, and good friction for consequential actions. Robinhood's agency states "less is more". Cash App frames its 2025 work as personality recalibration through a system. Revolut, Copilot, Chime and Mercury have published little or nothing in this area that was retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- **Monzo, "How we design magic moments" (Nov 2022):** "Whatever surprise and magic we want to design, we need a foundation of predictability in place first." Users should never be unsure "what a button will do". — [Monzo blog](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)
- Monzo: "attention is a form of currency, and if everything is a highlight then nothing is." — [Monzo blog, 2022](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)
- Monzo cites **Hick's law** ("the time needed to make a decision is proportional to the number of alternatives offered") and **affordance** ("users should never wonder whether an element is interactive or not"). — [Monzo blog, 2022](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)
- Monzo uses deliberate friction so people make "a fully considered choice to borrow money or make a big payment". Most moments should help people finish tasks and then "get out of people's way". Its design culture means "saying no to 1,000 things before saying yes". Delight examples: the Get Paid Early drag interaction, confetti, frost on card freeze, and thoughtful microcopy. — [Monzo blog, 2022](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)
- Monzo: "Simplicity isn't the absence of things, it's bringing order to complexity." — [Monzo blog, 2023](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen)
- A third-party summary of Monzo's product principles includes "optimise for learning with simple solutions" and "consistency and patterns build trust". It was not verified against Monzo's own site. — [principles.design](https://principles.design/examples/monzo-product-principles)
- **Robinhood / Porto Rocha:** "when it comes to standing out in a sea of fintech sameness, less is more", plus a "smart, modular system that could flex across their multiple finance products". — [Porto Rocha](https://www.portorocha.com/robinhood)
- **Cash App (2025):** design as "a core engine of our product strategy, not just a mere cosmetic improvement". The principles are "human-centered design, the reliability of automated technology, and a seamless interface". The statements are attributed to the company, not named designers. — [Design Compass, Dec 2025](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/12/03/cash-apps-new-design-language-and-ai-moneybot/)
- **Block banking design** (job posting): turn "financial and regulatory complexity into clear, trustworthy experiences". — [Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/job/principal-designer-banking/10883394)
- **Revolut 10** framing: key products on home, everything else "in just one tap". — [Revolut, 2023](https://www.revolut.com/news/revolut_launches_revolut_10_as_it_targets_primary_accounts_and_passes_35m_customers_worldwide)
- **Nubank (Oct 2023):** "NuDS promises a smaller learning curve and a cohesive experience, giving the app a familiar look & feel." It is "reducing the cognitive load on our customers", "with accessibility at its heart". — [Building Nu](https://building.nu.com/tailoring-products-for-fanatical-customers-in-various-countries/)

### Inferences
- **Turn Monzo's principles into CardMax checks:** (1) every tappable thing looks tappable and every non-tappable thing doesn't, so no pill-shaped decorative labels that look like buttons; (2) one highlight per screen; (3) delight (confetti, a card lift, a ticker) only on the one moment that matters per flow: the best card lifting in Wallet, the "total value back" reveal in Scan, and the hold-to-pay completion; (4) friction only on the consequential step (hold to pay), which already matches CardMax's simulated pay.
- **Cash App and Robinhood show personality coming from a few strong system choices** (one accent colour, one typeface, a consistent icon set, custom motion), not from more components. A hackathon team can copy that: one accent, one font family, one icon library at one stroke weight.

- The Cash App and Block design blog was not located. Statements come from press coverage and job postings.

## 6. Common fintech clutter anti-patterns and recommended fixes

### Takeaway
The documented failures are crowded first screens as products accumulate (Nubank users, Monzo's "overwhelming and confusing" early concepts and its cluttered lower half), over-hiding that makes things unfindable (Monzo hidden Pots), confusing reward units (Revolut multipliers), anxiety colours (red on balances, per Mercury's analysis), stacked sheets, too many disclosure levels, and blank or misleading empty states. NN/g's fixes are task-based prioritisation, two-level progressive disclosure, a purpose for every element, and empty states that report status and offer one next step. No Baymard or NN/g study specific to fintech clutter was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Minimalism is about removing elements that don't support user tasks, not a visual style.** Too few elements also hurts, because missing necessary content leaves users stuck. Visually sparse pages can still fail the heuristic. — [NN/g, 2021](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-minimalist-design/)
- NN/g's tips: limit decorative noise; don't overuse font or colour variation; avoid multipurpose visual cues; use progressive disclosure for less common tasks; give every element a purpose. Example: a countdown timer's "zen mode" shows only the remaining time and reveals controls on hover. — [NN/g, 2021](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-minimalist-design/)
- **Progressive-disclosure pitfalls:** too many options on the first display, confusing features up front, vague labels on progression controls, multiple ways to reach the same secondary options, splitting interdependent steps, and depth beyond two levels. — [NN/g, 2006](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)
- **Bottom-sheet pitfalls:** stacking sheets, relying on the grab handle alone, using sheets for complex content or for normal page flows. — [NN/g, 2023](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)
- **Empty states (NN/g, Sep 2021):** (1) use the empty state to communicate system status, for example "There are no records to display for the selected date range"; never show a misleading "No records" while data is still loading. (2) Provide in-context learning cues, such as DataDog's "Star your favorites to list them here". (3) Provide direct pathways, such as a "Create" button or a demo-data option. — [NN/g, Kate Kaplan, 2021](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)
- **Feature sprawl:** Monzo's app had "outgrown its own design", and early redesign concepts were "overwhelming and confusing". — [Monzo, 2023](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen). Nubank users cite a crowded first screen as products were added (anecdotal). — [Tecnoblog community](https://tecnoblog.net/comunidade/t/voces-acham-a-interface-do-nubank-facil/54207?tl=en)
- **Over-hiding:** Monzo beta users complained that hidden Pots were hard to find (behind a pencil icon), that the lower half of the screen felt cluttered, and that card access was clunky. — [Monzo Community, Dec 2022](https://community.monzo.com/t/app-evolution-now-in-monzo-labs/141702)
- **Confusing reward units:** users found Revolut's change from "×" multiplier to "per" wording confusing (anecdotal). — [Head for Points forum](https://www.headforpoints.com/forums/topic/revpoints-2/page/2) and Reddit (mirror, anecdotal). Robinhood's "3% cash back" is actually 3× points at 1¢. — [Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/robinhood-gold-card-not-really-3-percent-cash-back/)
- **Anxiety colour and promotional colour:** Mercury reportedly avoids red next to negative balances and bills, as well as "promotional oranges" and "engagement-bait purples". — [925Studios (third-party), 2026](https://www.925studios.co/blog/mercury-design-breakdown)
- **Rollout etiquette:** Monzo did not interrupt users mid-task with the redesign and let them snooze it. — [Monzo, 2023](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen)
- **Industry benchmark:** Forrester's review of US mobile banking apps found that digital and mobile-only banks tend to lead on UX design. — [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/forrester-reviews-and-scores-us-mobile-banking-apps-what-we-learned/)

### Inferences
These are CardMax decluttering rules synthesised from the sourced patterns above. They are not quotations.
1. **One hero number per screen.** Wallet: what the best card earns here. Scan: total value back. Deals: none, since the feed is the content and each row's value is its own number. Missed: total missed this period. Everything else uses body or caption size.
2. **One primary action per screen, as a filled button in the accent colour.** Secondary actions become text links, icon buttons or "…" menus. Never two full-width buttons stacked (for example "Scan again" under the result goes in the header as an icon or text button).
3. **Row anatomy (one pattern across Deals, Missed and Scan results):** a round logo or icon (no source gave a size; pick one value and reuse it everywhere), title (merchant or deal), **one** muted sub-line, right-aligned tabular amount. Allow at most one badge per row, and only when it carries state (for example "Best"), never for decoration or category.
4. **No explanatory sublines by default.** If text explains how the screen works, move it to a first-run empty state (NN/g's learning cue) or behind an info tap.
5. **Replace bordered boxes with spacing and grouping.** Use one surface level for grouped lists (an inset-grouped list) instead of stacked bordered cards. Borders and dividers only where alignment can't do the job.
6. **Two disclosure levels max.** Row → detail sheet. No sheet from a sheet, and every sheet has a visible close (X) and responds to Back.
7. **Rewards always in dollars.** Show the rate (3×, 5%) only as a secondary token next to the dollar figure.
8. **Colour budget:** neutral surfaces, one accent, green only for money gained, no red for "missed" (use neutral or amber), no rainbow category colours unless they encode something users need (as Apple Card's category colours do).
9. **Shared header:** the same large title style, position and right-side slot on all four tabs. This fixes the inconsistency between screens built by different people.
10. **Motion budget:** one signature animation per flow (card lift, value count-up, pay confirmation), with predictable transitions everywhere else, per Monzo's "predictability first".
11. **Empty states:** one sentence of status plus one direct action. For example, Missed with no data: "No purchases yet. Scan a receipt to start tracking." with a Scan button.

- No NN/g article specific to financial or banking app UX turned up in searches, though the general NN/g guidance above applies.
- No quantitative study (for example, task time or error rates) tying badge or pill density to usability in finance apps was found.
- Fintech UX "case studies" from agencies and Dribbble or Behance were not reviewed. Mobbin and Page Flows screen libraries require login and were not accessed, so screen-level details (element counts above the fold, exact row layouts, radii) remain unverified.

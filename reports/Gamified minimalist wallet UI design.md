# Calm the wallet, gamify the choice

CreditMaxx should look and behave like Apple Wallet with Monzo's discipline, and use its game layer for one thing only: rewarding the user for paying with the best card. The finance apps worth copying (Apple Wallet, Monzo, Revolut, Mercury and Apple Card) share one layout: a single main element, one primary action attached to it, a short activity list, and everything else one tap away. Their personality comes from a few deliberate choices (one accent color, one typeface, one icon set, purposeful motion) rather than from more components. The gamification evidence splits cleanly. Mechanics that reward a decision the user already wants to make, and that forgive lapses, hold up (Duolingo's streak freezes, Apple's Pause Rings). Mechanics tied to transaction volume or chance raised activity without improving decisions in the FCA's 9,140-person experiment, and they figured in a $7.5M Massachusetts settlement with Robinhood. A card optimizer is a rare case where playing well and acting in the user's interest are the same act, so a best-card streak with repair, a capture-rate score, and celebrations that state the dollar gain are defensible; leaderboards, spin wheels, XP per dollar and countdown deals are not. The teammates' Wallet feedback is mostly right: accept the "+" header button, the circular pay control, the Apple-style card detail with history and a "…" menu, the pay control between card and history, and the removal of the store gate, but reshape "why this card won" into one line under the card with the full breakdown in a sheet rather than moving the whole box above it. No maintained open-source Apple Wallet clone exists for the web, so build on the Motion library already installed; the new packages worth adding (canvas-confetti, NumberFlow, optionally Radix Dialog and ios-haptics) total under 30 KB gzipped, and the repo already has a sheet, an SVG chart and a Web Audio chime. The biggest visible gain per hour of work is one shared set of design tokens and one header across all four tabs, followed by restructuring the Wallet.

*How to read this report.* Facts from the research carry inline source links. Recommendations that the sources don't directly establish are marked *(inference)*. Statements about the current code come from reading the repository and name a file path rather than a URL. The coordinator's 390×844 screenshot audit is used only to describe today's UI.

## Five finance apps share one layout: one main element, one action, a short list

The best-documented home-screen redesigns arrive at the same order: the user's financial state first, then a short activity list, with controls one level down. Monzo spent "over a year" rebuilding its home screen for "more than 8 million people", running "4 major and 2 minor experiments" with over 1,000 customers after early concepts tested as "overwhelming and confusing" ([Monzo](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen)). The team kept three core jobs on the surface (viewing balances, copying card details, searching transactions) and defined its goal plainly: **"Simplicity isn't the absence of things, it's bringing order to complexity"** ([Monzo](https://www.monzo.com/blog/how-we-built-the-new-home-screen)). Revolut 10 (October 2023) kept key products on the home screen and put everything else "in just one tap" ([Revolut](https://www.revolut.com/news/revolut_launches_revolut_10_as_it_targets_primary_accounts_and_passes_35m_customers_worldwide)). A third-party breakdown sums up Mercury the same way: "Users see their financial state first, then their controls" ([925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/mercury-design-breakdown)). At launch, tapping Apple Card showed the balance, the payment due date, recent transactions and one button for detailed activity ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/news/apple-card-the-macstories-overview/)). None of these home screens puts explanations, several stat blocks or promotional tiles above the activity list. CreditMaxx's Wallet does: a long explanation box, a store chip, a checkout line and a large outlined button all compete with the card.

Apple Wallet is the most direct model because CreditMaxx already imitates it, and Apple's current documentation (iOS 27, with support pages dated September 14, 2026) describes its anatomy clearly. The home screen has three header controls, **Add Card ("+"), Search and More ("…")** ([Apple, iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-cards-and-passes-iph9b7f53382/27.0/ios); [Apple, iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/manage-passes-cards-and-keys-iphbea35dbc4/27.0/ios)). The default card sits at the front of the stack, and in the collapsed stack only each card's top strip shows, so the issuer logo at top-left and one key value identify each card ([HIG: Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet)). "Tap an item to see its details": the detail view shows the card with a **rewards or balance line below it**, and recent activity further down ("scroll down to see recent activity", older transactions grouped by month) ([Apple, iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/manage-passes-cards-and-keys-iphbea35dbc4/27.0/ios); [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120479)). A More or Info button, which third-party help pages place at top right, leads to Card Details and then Remove Card ([iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/ios-26-wallet-app-new-features/)). When paying in a store, the default card appears, the other cards wait at the bottom of the screen, action buttons such as **"Pay In Full", "Use Rewards" and "Pay Later" sit beneath the card**, and the user holds the phone near the reader "until Done or a checkmark appears" ([Apple, iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/make-purchases-with-apple-pay-iphbd4cf42b4/27.0/ios); [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120477)).

One point matters for CreditMaxx's pitch: **Apple does not choose a card by merchant or by reward**. Location-based card selection exists only in a 2021 Apple patent ([NFCW](https://www.nfcw.com/?p=71612)), so the moment where the best card lifts for this store is CreditMaxx's own feature, not a copy. Apple's design guidelines (the HIG) add three constraints. Custom controls must not show "Apple Pay" or its logo ([HIG: Apple Pay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay)). Liquid Glass, Apple's translucent material, belongs only on floating controls, never on content ([HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). And a view should have "one or two" prominent buttons, set apart by "style — not size" ([HIG: Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)).

The clearest way to present rewards is **in dollars, attached to the transaction**. Apple Card shows the Daily Cash earned on each purchase in that purchase's detail view and totals it by week, month and year ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT209227)). Points and multipliers confuse people: Robinhood marketed its Gold Card as "3% cash back", but the card actually earns 3× points worth 1¢ each ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-gold-card-rewards); [Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/robinhood-gold-card-not-really-3-percent-cash-back/)). Cash App Boosts limit the choice to one active offer at a time ([CreditCards.com](https://www.creditcards.com/card-advice/cash-app-boost/)), and Monzo designs so users are "never more than one-click away from a purchase breakdown" ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)). CreditMaxx's best-card pill currently leads with "4x · 7.2% yield", which is two rate formats and no dollar figure. The headline should be "+$3.06", with the multiplier as a quiet secondary label *(inference)*.

These brands get their personality from a few strong choices rather than from more interface. Robinhood's 2024 refresh by the agency Porto Rocha concluded that "less is more" in "a sea of fintech sameness", using black, white and "mature neutrals" with one accent, Robin Neon, "used in pops" ([Porto Rocha](https://www.portorocha.com/robinhood)). Cash App's late-2025 update brought a brighter fluorescent green, a proprietary typeface (Cash Sans), more than 650 new icons and custom motion, framed as a recalibration rather than a redesign ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/12/03/cash-apps-new-design-language-and-ai-moneybot/)). Mercury reportedly keeps red and yellow for errors and warnings only and avoids red next to negative balances because red causes anxiety ([925Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/mercury-design-breakdown), third-party). Even Duolingo, the most playful reference, got cleaner in its February 2026 core-tabs refresh: it cut type styles to a minimal set, separated sections with whitespace instead of containers, and dropped header art that "took space without communicating anything useful" ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/core-tabs-redesign/)). Monzo's principle covers the rest: "attention is a form of currency, and if everything is a highlight then nothing is" ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)).

| App | What makes it work (sourced) | What CreditMaxx takes |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Wallet | Header "+", Search, "…"; card front and center; status line below the card; actions beneath the card; "Done" plus a checkmark | The whole Wallet tab structure, the card detail order, the success state |
| Apple Card | Daily Cash in dollars per transaction, plus totals per period | Rewards in dollars first; per-purchase reward plus a period total on Missed |
| Monzo | Order rather than removal; one highlight; predictability before delight; breakdown one click away | Explanations behind a tap; one highlight per screen |
| Revolut 10 / Mercury | Key products on home, the rest one tap away; state first, controls second | Main element first, controls in the header or a "…" menu |
| Robinhood / Cash App | Neutral base, one accent, one typeface, custom icons and motion | One accent, one rounded font, one icon set |
| Duolingo (2026) | Rounded shapes everywhere, few type styles, whitespace instead of boxes | One rounded shape family; play in motion and wording, not in decoration |

The research has gaps. Copilot Money was a 2024 Apple Design Award finalist for Innovation ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/)), but no first-party account of its design reasoning turned up, and nothing was found on Chime's current design. Cash App's home layout, the "smaller cents" balance treatment, and exact corner radii and spacing for any of these apps are unverified. CreditMaxx should copy these apps' principles, not their exact pixels.

## Gamify the decision, never the spending

The strongest evidence from money apps shows that game mechanics raise activity, which is not the same as better outcomes. A meta-analysis found only small effects of gamification on behavior (g = .25) and motivation (g = .36), and those effects held up less well in rigorous studies than the effect on learning (g = .49) ([Sailer & Homner](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)). The UK FCA's 2024 experiment, with **9,140 participants trading real money**, tested four features. Push notifications raised the number of trades by **11%** and points with a prize draw raised it by **12%**, and those two also raised the share of risky trades by 8% and 6%. A trader leaderboard and flashing prices had no significant effect on how many trades people made. Participants with low financial literacy, and those aged 18–34, reacted more strongly. The authors concluded that the features "attracted consumer attention, while conveying no additional information which could improve trading" ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)). The same note cites an Ontario study in which points worth eight cents or less raised trading by 39% ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)).

Regulators and users have already drawn the line. Robinhood stopped showing confetti for customer "firsts" on March 31, 2021 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-03-31/robinhood-ditches-its-confetti-animation-following-criticism)), and in January 2024 it paid **$7.5M** to settle a Massachusetts complaint. Press coverage reports that the settlement requires it to stop celebratory imagery tied to how often customers trade and to drop features that mimic games of chance ([ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices)); the consent order itself was not retrieved. The FTC's 2022 report on dark patterns lists false scarcity, countdown timers, and comparison sites that claim neutrality while selling placement ([Hunton](https://www.huntonprivacyblog.com/2022/09/27/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns/)). The CFPB warned in 2024 that credit-card comparison tools break the law when they steer users toward products because of kickbacks ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-issues-guidance-to-rein-in-rigged-comparison-shopping-results-for-credit-cards-and-other-financial-products/)); it withdrew that guidance in May 2025, though its underlying power over unfair or deceptive practices remains ([Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/05/16/cfpb-rescinds-67-guidance-documents/)). PayPal Honey shows what that trust failure costs: in December 2024 it was accused of favoring partner coupons over better ones ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/23/paypal-honey-browser-extension-controversy/)) and reportedly lost more than 7 million users ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/honey-loses-over-7-million-users-and-7000-stores-following-youtubers-viral-expose-3397619/)). Tone matters too. Monzo's year-end spending recap drew an "outpouring of hand-wringing shame and anger" for naming people's everyday habits such as fast food and takeaway deliveries ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/43457/monzo-wrapped-prompts-outpouring-of-shame-and-anger)), and one customer took it to the Financial Ombudsman ([Freevacy/Guardian](https://www.freevacy.com/news/the-guardian/monzo-under-fire-over-misuse-of-personal-data/7195)); Cleo's teasing "roast mode", by contrast, is something users switch on themselves ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/want-to-make-your-ai-chatbot-funny-hiring-comedians-is-a-start)). None of these rules legally binds a simulated hackathon demo, but they describe what judges and users read as manipulative *(inference)*.

The mechanics that hold up are the ones that forgive mistakes. In 2020 Duolingo changed its streak so that a single lesson keeps it alive, separate from the daily goal, and Day-14 retention rose by a relative **3.3%** ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Letting learners hold two "streak freezes" at once *increased* daily active learners by 0.38%, a milestone animation raised new learners' Day-7 retention by 1.7%, and Duolingo admits that fear of losing a streak can stop people from starting at all ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/)). A 2023 *Journal of Consumer Research* paper found that a broken streak lowers later engagement and that being able to "repair" it softens the drop ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)), and Apple added Pause Rings in watchOS 11 so a rest day doesn't break an Activity streak ([iMore](https://www.imore.com/health-fitness/apple-watch/how-to-pause-activity-streaks-in-watchos-11)). Badges steer people toward whatever they reward, risky or safe, according to research the FCA cites ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf)). And people avoid looking at their finances when they expect bad news, the "ostrich effect" ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23945.pdf)), so a large, growing "missed" number can drive users away instead of motivating them.

Savings and cashback apps show the same split. Qapital's user-set "rules" and Acorns' round-ups automate a habit the user already wants, moving small amounts into savings or investments as people spend ([Decode Agency](https://decode.agency/article/fintech-app-gamification-examples/); [StriveCloud](https://www.strivecloud.io/blog/fintech-gamification-examples), both vendor blogs). Long Game went further and turned saving into coins for mini-games with cash prizes ([PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8046219); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3065837/the-long-game-wants-to-make-saving-money-as-fun-as-buying-a-lottery-ticket)). Prize-linked saving has some support: in South African bank data, people who chose it raised total savings by about 1% of annual income ([Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4047)). Truist bought Long Game in 2022 ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/40178/truist-buys-savings-gamification-app-long-game)), and a single competitor blog says it was pulled from app stores in December 2023, which is unverified ([Beem](https://trybeem.com/blog/?p=305093)). The closest cousin to CreditMaxx is Fetch, a receipt-scanning cashback app, which ran a once-a-day reward wheel and replaced it in January 2024 with points-earning games, drawing "Bring back the daily spin" complaints ([Coupons in the News](https://couponsinthenews.com/2024/01/08/fetch-introduces-games-for-points-pleasing-some-and-irking-others/)). Chance can be aimed at a good habit like saving, but CreditMaxx's underlying behavior is spending, and chance-based rewards attached to transactions raised transaction volume in the FCA study *(inference)*.

The direct competitors present savings as a utility. Kudos, MaxRewards and CardPointers show dashboards and perk trackers, and none showed visible gamification in the sources retrieved, although Kudos uses loss-based marketing ("take back $300 in missed credit card perks") ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kudos-put-your-wallet-to-work/id6443548838)). That leaves an opening, and CreditMaxx has an unusual advantage: the move that wins the game, using the best card, is also the move that serves the user, so a game layer here doesn't have to work against the user's interest *(inference)*.

| Mechanic | Verdict | Evidence | Build now? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capture-rate ring** ("82% of possible rewards captured") as the main figure on Missed, with the dollars missed beneath it | Build | Pairs a positive number with the loss; ostrich effect; Apple's ring metaphor | Yes. For the Missed tab specifically, the evidence supports presenting missed rewards as **recoverable, not shameful** *(inference)*: lead with the positive score and put the dollar figure below it, describe each cause neutrally ("the card used didn't earn the dining bonus"), attach one fix ("Use Amex Gold for dining"), and use neither red nor mocking merchant copy. The current headline, "$69.75 more in rewards over the last 30 days" on a warm brown card, is a loss figure with no score beside it and no next step.

## Motion covers the hard parts; new packages add under 30 KB gzipped

No maintained, properly licensed web clone of Apple Wallet is worth forking. The GitHub results are a 2019 MIT component that predates React hooks ([react-apple-wallet](https://github.com/elainegasca/react-apple-wallet)), unlicensed demos with a handful of stars, and React Native projects ([tarikfp](https://github.com/tarikfp/react-native-apple-wallet-animation)). Motion's own documentation already describes the technique CreditMaxx needs. `layout` animates any position change caused by a re-render, `layoutId` with `AnimatePresence` morphs one element into another, and `LayoutGroup` coordinates sibling elements. Corner radius and shadow stay crisp during a morph only when set through `style`, and `scrollbar-gutter: stable` prevents stray layout jumps ([Motion docs](https://motion.dev/docs/react-layout-animations)). Motion's App Store tutorial for the card-to-detail morph is paywalled; the free MIT copy-and-paste references are Motion Primitives' Morphing Dialog and Cult UI's Expandable Card ([Motion Primitives](https://github.com/ibelick/motion-primitives); [Cult UI](https://github.com/nolly-studio/cult-ui)). The hold-to-pay circle also has to be built by hand. The best free references are Emil Kowalski's hold-to-delete write-up, which fills over 2 s at constant speed and snaps back in 200 ms on release ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-hold-to-delete-component)), and Motion's hold-to-confirm approach, where a `useMotionValue` progress value drives an SVG ring through `pathLength` ([Motion](https://motion.dev/tutorials/react-hold-to-confirm), full code paywalled). The hold-button components on 21st.dev have unknown licenses, so use them for reference only.

The repo already contains most of the pieces. `StorePicker.tsx` is a hand-built Motion bottom sheet with a grab handle and a `#1c1c1e` surface; `DailyChart.tsx` is a plain SVG bar chart; `feedback.ts` plays a three-note Web Audio chime and calls `navigator.vibrate`; `Odometer.tsx` and `CountUp.tsx` are two separate animated counters; and `PayButton.tsx` already draws a `pathLength` ring and supports holding a key instead of a finger. The work is mostly consolidation, not adding libraries. The research team measured the sizes below with esbuild and gzip on 2026-10-10, excluding React; Motion's cost of about 45–52 KB gzipped is already paid.

Check licenses before copying anything. Magic UI, Motion Primitives and Cult UI are MIT and already use Tailwind 4 with `motion/react` ([Magic UI](https://github.com/magicuidesign/magicui)), with two cautions: Motion Primitives lists Motion 11 in its own package file, so its components are untested on Motion 14, and Cult UI's Family Drawer is built on the unmaintained Vaul. React Bits and Animate UI use "MIT + Commons Clause", which is fine inside an app but bars redistributing the components themselves ([React Bits](https://github.com/DavidHDev/react-bits)). Origin UI now lives in the coss.com/ui repo, whose root license is AGPL-3.0, although the old Origin folder in that repo is still MIT ([coss](https://github.com/cosscom/coss)). Aceternity, Hover.dev and Skiper are mostly paid or restrict redistribution. For the playful look, neobrutalism-components and 8bitcn are maintained, MIT and built for Tailwind 4 ([neobrutalism-components](https://github.com/ekmas/neobrutalism-components); [8bitcn](https://github.com/TheOrcDev/8bitcn-ui)), but treat them as style references only, because adopting either wholesale would clash with the minimalist goal *(inference)*.

Keep expectations for sound and haptics realistic. Safari has never supported the Vibration API ([MDN BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/Navigator.json)); the web workaround toggles a hidden switch-style checkbox to make the iPhone vibrate. Developer reports in two independent repos say iOS 26.5 stopped haptics triggered by code ([ios-haptics #8](https://github.com/tijnjh/ios-haptics/issues/8); [web-haptics issues](https://github.com/lochie/web-haptics/issues)), though Apple has not confirmed the change, and ios-haptics v3.1+ works around it by passing the user's real tap through a transparent overlay. On an iPhone, then, a vibration can fire when the finger first presses down, but almost certainly not when a 1.6 s hold *completes* *(inference from how the library works; untested on a device)*. Web Audio on iOS only plays after it is unlocked inside a user gesture ([Matt Montag](https://www.mattmontag.com/web/unlock-web-audio-in-safari-for-ios-and-macos)), which `feedback.ts` already does on press-down. The checkmark, the "+$3.06" and the chime have to carry the moment on the judges' phones; any vibration is a bonus.

## Twelve rules and four screen plans strip out the bloat

### Twelve rules that remove the bloat

The bloat comes less from features than from four different visual styles plus explanations shown inline, and the code confirms it. The frontend uses **35 distinct raw hex colors across 8 files** (some legitimately for card art) plus five Tailwind accent colors: emerald, sky, red, amber and cyan. `screens/missed/missed.css` defines its own font, its own amber-and-brown palette and its own button styles. The reduced-motion setting (`MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"`) is applied separately on Wallet, Scan and Deals but not on Missed. NN/g's minimalism guideline is the right test: remove elements that don't help the user's task, and "don't overuse font or color variation" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-minimalist-design/)). Nubank describes the consistency its design system brings as "reducing the cognitive load on our customers" ([Building Nu](https://building.nu.com/tailoring-products-for-fanatical-customers-in-various-countries/)).

| # | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One main number per screen.** Wallet: dollars this card earns here. Scan: total value back. Deals: none (each row carries its own). Missed: capture %. | Inference from Monzo, Apple Card, Mercury |
| 2 | **One prominent action per screen**, set apart by style rather than size. Secondary actions become text links, header icons or "…" menu items. | [HIG: Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons) |
| 3 | **Dollars first, rates second.** "+$3.06 · 4×", never "7.2% yield" as the headline. | [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT209227); [Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/robinhood-gold-card-not-really-3-percent-cash-back/) |
| 4 | **One row layout everywhere:** round icon, title, one muted second line, amount right-aligned in fixed-width digits, and at most one status badge. | Inference (no source describes row layout) |
| 5 | **Spacing instead of bordered boxes.** One raised surface color for grouped lists and sheets. | [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/core-tabs-redesign/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-minimalist-design/) |

| 12 | **Limited motion:** press feedback on everything tappable, one signature animation per flow, nothing looping on idle screens, and a reduced-motion version of each animation. | [Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo); [Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations); [Rauno](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design) |

The design tokens that put these rules into practice fit in one `@theme` block in `frontend/src/index.css`. **Color:** keep the pure-black background, which suits OLED screens and is already the base, and add one or two lighter surface colors for raised elements, such as the `#1c1c1e` that `StorePicker` already uses. Material's Android guidance recommends dark grey rather than black ([Material Components](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Dark.md)), so keeping black is a deliberate choice *(inference)*. Choose one saturated brand accent for the pay ring, the best-card highlight and "+$" amounts, and retire the cyan, sky and Missed browns *(inference)*. Contrast ratios computed with the [WCAG 2.2 formula](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum) set the terms: saturated greens and blues stand out strongly against black (Duolingo's #58CC02 measures **10.05:1**), but white text *on* those colors fails at 2.09:1, so filled accent buttons need near-black text, and mid-greys such as #4B4B4B fail as text on black at 2.41:1.

**Type:** use one rounded font family, `ui-rounded, "Nunito Variable", system-ui`. `ui-rounded` gives Apple's SF Rounded but works only in Safari ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/extended-system-fonts)). Nunito's digits are all the same width at every weight tested, so amounts line up, whereas Fredoka and Quicksand have digits of varying widths and no fixed-width option and should stay away from numbers ([Google Fonts sources](https://github.com/google/fonts/tree/main/ofl)). Two weights and about four sizes are enough *(inference)*. **Shape:** use one family of corner radii and round every edge, following Duolingo's rule that "pointy shapes are off-brand" ([Duolingo](https://design.duolingo.com/illustration), quoted from a search-indexed copy because the official page now redirects), including the bars in the Missed chart.

**Game feel** should come from motion, not decoration. Apple's guidance on spring animations says a bounce of 0 is the safe default, around 0.15 feels brisk, about 0.3 is noticeably bouncy, and anything above roughly 0.4 can feel exaggerated ([WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/)). That maps directly onto Motion's `visualDuration` and `bounce`: a card lift of about 0.4–0.5 s with a bounce of 0–0.15, and more bounce only when a winning card or a receipt arrives *(inference)*. Always set bounce explicitly, because the installed Motion 14.0.0 code defaults to 0.3 while the [docs page](https://motion.dev/docs/react-transitions) says 0.25. Keep routine transitions under 300 ms ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations)) and use `whileTap={{ scale: 0.97 }}` for press feedback. The one playful flourish worth its cost is a Duolingo-style chunky button, used only for each screen's single primary action: a solid, darker "edge" sits under the face, and the face drops onto it when pressed. Josh Comeau's version animates only `transform`, pressing down in 34 ms and springing back over 600 ms ([Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/animation/3d-button/)).

**Reduced motion** needs two fixes. Move `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"` up to `App.tsx` so it covers every screen. Then revisit the global `prefers-reduced-motion` rule in `index.css`, which cuts *every* CSS transition and animation to zero. That is fine for decoration, but a hold-progress fill built in CSS would then complete instantly, and the hold ring is essential feedback that must keep filling under reduced motion, so drive it from a Motion value or exempt it from the rule *(inference)*. Supporting reduced motion is a WCAG AAA requirement rather than AA ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)), but it costs little here.

### Scan: one total, two rows, one question

Before a scan, the screen should offer one chunky primary action, "Scan a cart", with "Try a sample cart" as a plain text link and the manual store-and-subtotal form behind an "Enter manually" link, revealed step by step as NN/g recommends ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)) *(inference)*. Today three numbered boxes (Coupon → Card → Total) lead the result; lead instead with the main number, "Total value back", counting up once as this screen's single celebration. Beneath it, the coupon and the card become two rows in the shared row layout, and their arithmetic moves into a "Why?" sheet. The six-row "Which card did you pay with?" picker is the heaviest element on the screen. Replace it with one question that defaults to the recommended card ("Paid with Amex Gold?"), a primary "Log it" button and a secondary "I used another card" that reveals the list, cutting the choice down the way Cash App's one-offer-at-a-time rule does ([CreditCards.com](https://www.creditcards.com/card-advice/cash-app-boost/)). The "Log purchase" button must look enabled when it is enabled, since Monzo's rule is that users should never have to wonder whether something is interactive ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo)). The amber "Coupons are simulated · not verified offers" banner becomes a muted "Sample coupon" line on the coupon row, and "Read by Gemini" and "Sample cart" become plain second-line text instead of pills.

### Deals: dollars first, ranking explained, no countdowns

Deal rows should show dollars first: the merchant name, a second line reading "Coupon + Amex Gold", and "$9.73 back" right-aligned, with the rate small and rounded ("30%") rather than "30.41%" *(inference from rule 3)*. Remove the explanatory paragraph and replace it with one quiet line saying how deals are ranked, such as "Ranked by dollars back on a typical order · demo coupons". That matches the list-disclosure requirement reported in the Massachusetts settlement and addresses the steering concern behind the CFPB guidance ([ThinkAdvisor](https://thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/18/robinhood-to-pay-7-5m-over-gamification-practices); [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-issues-guidance-to-rein-in-rigged-comparison-shopping-results-for-credit-cards-and-other-financial-products/)). Keep the Meals/Clothing/Travel tabs, but use emoji or icons consistently, with tap targets at least 44 px tall. Never add urgency such as countdowns, "only 3 left" or "expires tonight", which fall into the FTC's false-scarcity and countdown-timer categories ([Hunton](https://www.huntonprivacyblog.com/2022/09/27/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns/)). Tapping a deal already opens the same stack in Scan, and the existing end-to-end test (`e2e/scan-deals.mjs`) covers that handoff.

### Missed: lead with the score, not the loss

Missed needs the most visual work, because it looks like a different app. Delete `missed.css` in favor of the shared tokens and header, and drop the "EVERY CARD COUNTS" label. The main figure becomes a capture-rate ring ("82% captured · last 30 days") with "$69.75 left on the table" on a second line, plus one practical fix ("Dining: use Amex Gold") and, once it exists, a small streak chip ("5 best-card picks in a row") *(inference, from the mechanics table)*. Keep the SVG chart but restyle it with rounded bar tops, the single accent color, and today's bar highlighted. Purchase rows follow the shared layout, for example "Chipotle" with a second line "Used Debit · Best: Amex Gold" and a muted, right-aligned "$1.80 missed", while purchases made with the best card get a small check instead of "$0.00". This mirrors Apple Card's two levels, an amount on each purchase plus a total for the period ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT209227)). Avoid red throughout, and don't put a count badge on the Missed tab, since a count there adds anxiety *(inference)*. The tab name "Missed" itself frames the screen as a loss; "Rewards" or "Recap" would match a score-first design, but that is a team decision rather than a research finding.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we add game elements to a banking app" to "where does CreditMaxx's one game moment happen?" The answer is the instant the user pays with the best card and sees how many dollars that earned. Everything the evidence condemns (rewarding volume, chance, scarcity, shaming) rewards something other than that decision, and everything it supports (forgiving streaks, celebrations that carry information, progress framed as gains) reinforces it. That sets CreditMaxx apart from other gamified finance products, because its score doubles as proof that its advice is honest: the capture rate motivates the user and also shows that the recommended card really was the best one, and the CFPB guidance and the Honey backlash show that this kind of proof is what earns trust in a comparison tool.

The redesign is mostly about taking things away. The repo already has the sheet, the chart, the chime, the counter and the ring; what it lacks is one shared set of design tokens and the discipline to keep explanations one tap away. Two uncertainties remain. Apple publishes no measurements or timings for the Wallet card stack, so spring settings and card overlap are judgment calls to tune on a real phone. And web vibration on current iPhones is unreliable, so every celebration has to work without it.
